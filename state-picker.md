# How Founders Actually Choose a State

The honest version — no state's marketing pitch, just the trade-offs.

> **Fee disclaimer:** State filing fees change. The figures below are 2026 numbers. Confirm the current fee on the state's official business portal before filing.

## The honest breakdown

**Wyoming — the default pick for most non-US founders.**
Low fees, strong privacy (member names stay off the public filing), business-friendly courts, and no state income tax. If you're running SaaS, e-commerce, or freelancing through a US LLC, Wyoming is usually the answer.

**New Mexico — the quiet bargain.**
$50 filing and no annual report requirement at all. The trade-off: less of an established business-court track record than Wyoming or Delaware, and some banks/processors are less familiar with NM entities. Great if you want the cheapest compliant option and plan to keep things simple.

**Delaware — the VC pick.**
If you're raising from US venture funds, Delaware C-corp is the industry standard — investors expect it. But for a plain LLC that isn't raising VC, Delaware's annual tax makes it more expensive than Wyoming for no practical benefit. Don't pay the Delaware premium unless you need it.

**Your home state — the nexus rule.**
If you physically operate, hire, or hold inventory in a specific state, you'll likely need to register ("foreign qualify") there anyway. Forming elsewhere doesn't dodge that — it just adds a second registration. Real presence beats clever paperwork.

## What founders get wrong

- Picking Delaware "because startups do" without a fundraising plan. That's a yearly tax bill for nothing.
- Picking purely on filing fee and ignoring the annual cost. A $50 filing with a $300 annual tax is not cheaper than a $102 filing with a $60 annual fee.
- Forming in one state and operating in another without foreign qualification. That's not a loophole — it's a compliance gap.
