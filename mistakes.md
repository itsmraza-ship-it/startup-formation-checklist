# The 10 Formation Mistakes Founders Actually Make

Learned the hard way by other people's companies. Read this before you file.

## 1. Filing in Delaware without a fundraising plan
Delaware is the VC standard — for C-corps raising from US funds. For a non-resident LLC with no fundraising plans, it's just a higher annual tax bill than Wyoming or New Mexico. Match the state to the plan.

## 2. Skipping the registered agent (or using a friend's address)
Every state requires a registered agent with a physical in-state address, and that address is public record. Using a friend's apartment puts their address on the internet and creates a mess when they move.

## 3. Commingling funds
Paying for groceries from the business account — or running client revenue through your personal account — can pierce your liability protection. The LLC protects you only if you treat it like a separate entity. Separate accounts from day one.

## 4. Getting the EIN late
Non-US founders without an SSN apply for an EIN by fax or mail, which takes weeks. Everything downstream — Stripe, PayPal, bank accounts — waits on the EIN. Start the EIN process the moment your Articles are filed.

## 5. No Operating Agreement
"It's just me, I don't need one." Then a co-founder joins, or a dispute arises, or a bank asks for it during onboarding. Single-member or not, put ownership, decision-making, and what happens if someone leaves in writing.

## 6. Ignoring annual compliance deadlines
Annual reports and franchise taxes have hard deadlines and real penalties — some states administratively dissolve companies that fall too far behind. Put every deadline on a calendar the day you form. (We built a free [GitHub Action](../compliance-reminder-action) that opens reminder issues automatically.)

## 7. Forgetting Form 5472
A foreign-owned single-member US LLC is a "disregarded entity" for tax — but the IRS still requires information reporting: a pro-forma Form 1120 with Form 5472, every year. The penalty for missing it is steep ($25,000 per year as of recent IRS guidance — verify current). This is not tax advice; get a cross-border tax professional.

## 8. Assuming the LLC means zero taxes
"No US taxes" is not the same as "no filings." Depending on your structure and activity, you may owe 1040-NR, 1065, state filings, or information returns. Budget for professional tax help from year one.

## 9. Operating in a state without foreign qualification
Forming in Wyoming but running a warehouse or office in Texas? You likely need to register as a foreign entity in Texas too. Forming in a "cheap" state doesn't exempt you from the states where you actually operate.

## 10. Treating formation as the finish line
Filing the Articles is step one of about thirty. The founders who thrive treat formation as the start of a compliance habit: separate books, filed reports, paid taxes, documented decisions. The paperwork is the business.
