# The Startup Formation Checklist

Work through this in order. Check each box only when it's actually done.

---

## Phase 1 — Pre-formation (before you file anything)

- [ ] **Decide the entity type.** For most non-US founders, a US LLC is the simplest fit. Planning to raise VC from US investors? A Delaware C-corp may be the better vehicle — talk to a startup lawyer before you commit.
- [ ] **Pick your state** (see [state-picker.md](state-picker.md)). Wyoming, New Mexico, Delaware, or your home state — each has real trade-offs.
- [ ] **Search your company name.** Check the state's business entity database for conflicts. Also check domain availability and app-store name availability.
- [ ] **Choose a registered agent.** Every state requires one with a physical in-state address. Don't use a friend's apartment — your agent address is public record.
- [ ] **Line up a US mailing address.** Separate from your registered agent. Banks, Stripe, and the IRS all want somewhere to reach you.
- [ ] **Get your documents ready.** Passport copy, and proof of address (utility bill or bank statement) — you'll need these for EIN, banking, and payment processors.

## Phase 2 — Formation (the filing)

- [ ] **File Articles of Organization** with the state. This creates the LLC. Fees vary by state (Wyoming $102, New Mexico $50, Delaware $90 — 2026 figures, confirm current before filing).
- [ ] **Get your EIN** (Employer Identification Number) from the IRS. Non-US founders without an SSN apply by fax/mail — it takes longer, so start early. You need an EIN before Stripe, PayPal, or a US bank account.
- [ ] **Draft an Operating Agreement.** Single member? Still do it. Multi-member? Absolutely do it — ownership splits, decision rights, and buyout terms belong in writing, not in a handshake.
- [ ] **Hold your organizational meeting / consent.** Document the first decisions (officers, bank account authorization, fiscal year) in writing.

## Phase 3 — Post-formation (where founders actually win or lose)

- [ ] **Open a US business bank account.** Approval is subject to the bank's decision — no one can guarantee it. Bring your EIN letter, Articles, Operating Agreement, and passport. We'll guide you through the process.
- [ ] **Set up Stripe / PayPal.** You'll need the EIN + US bank account. Application approval is the processor's call, not ours.
- [ ] **Separate your money on day one.** Business income goes to the business account. Commingling personal and business funds is the fastest way to lose your liability protection.
- [ ] **Set up basic bookkeeping.** A spreadsheet works at the start; accounting software works better. Track every dollar from day one — future-you doing taxes will be grateful.
- [ ] **Calendar your annual compliance.** Annual reports, franchise taxes, and registered agent renewals have hard deadlines with real penalties. Miss them and the state can dissolve your company. See our [compliance-reminder-action](../compliance-reminder-action) repo for a free GitHub Action that nags you before deadlines.
- [ ] **Understand your tax filings.** A foreign-owned single-member US LLC is typically a "disregarded entity" for US tax — but it still has IRS reporting obligations (including Form 5472 with a pro-forma Form 1120). Multi-member LLCs file Form 1065. This is not tax advice — get a cross-border tax professional.
- [ ] **Get any licenses you need.** Depending on your business and state, you may need a business license or seller's permit. Check your state's requirements.
